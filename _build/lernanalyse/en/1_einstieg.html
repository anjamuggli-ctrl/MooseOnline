Introduction

Starting point: What does this result tell me?

“She got a 3.5 out of 6.”
“He just didn’t get it.”
“Everyone worked really well today.”

Statements like these sum up a lot and reveal little. A grade does not show how someone thought, and a quiet classroom does not show whether anyone learned. This page asks: What actually is learning, how can I recognize it, and how do I use what I see for the next step?

Line of thought · three questions for any analysis
1
What do I see? Approaches, results, changes over time, described as precisely as possible. → Observing
2
How can it be explained? Test several interpretations: the person, the task, the teaching, the wider context. → Assessment–support cycle
3
What follows from it? For the students, for my teaching and for working with others. → Fit

Guiding idea

Learning happens in the mind and is never directly visible. All we see are traces: actions, statements, products and changes. Analyzing learning means collecting these traces carefully, interpreting them cautiously and checking the interpretation in class. The goal is not a verdict on a person but a better-matched learning opportunity.
