Toolbox: learning conversations in everyday teaching

Many good learning conversations last only a few minutes. The following tools can be used without much preparation (own synthesis from the sources).

Three-minute GROW

  • “What do you want to have finished today?”
  • “Where are you right now?”
  • “What could you do now?”
  • “What will you do first?”
after Kremers & Schlüter, 2018

Scale in passing

  • have them show 1 to 5 fingers
  • “What helps you already be at 3?”
  • “What would you need for a 4?”
after Berg & Shilts, 2005

One compliment per lesson

  • describe specifically what someone did
  • say what effect it had
  • don’t combine it with criticism
after Berg & Shilts, 2005; Lötscher et al., 2023

Ask back

  • “What went well for you?”
  • “What are you unsure about?”
  • only then give feedback
after Lötscher et al., 2023, p. 110

A mistake in four sentences

  • appreciation
  • mistake and cause
  • fixable through own effort
  • confidence and help
after Wisniewski & Zierer, 2017

Good news home

  • one specific positive observation
  • as a short message to parents
  • to someone different each week
after Berg & Shilts, 2005

Tools to download

Eight tools for teaching practice and the classroom, as an editable Word file or as a PDF to print. They are all collected in one file and may be freely used and adapted. The documents are in German.

1 · Learning conversation guide

Preparation, setting, questions for each GROW phase, space for notes and an agreement with a follow-up date.

2 · Question cards “Asking solution-focused questions”

24 cards to cut out: goal, exception, scale, resources, other people’s perspective and next step.

3 · Feedback planner

Grid with the levels task, process and self-regulation and the directions feed back, feed up and feed forward, plus a self-observation.

4 · “Ich schaffs” plan for adolescents

Worksheet for students: from the skill to helpers and a step-by-step plan to the log and the celebration.

5 · Preparing critical feedback

Planning sheet in eight steps: from the specific behavior to the wish and the benefits to the trial run and follow-up talk.

6 · WOWW observation sheet and class scale

List “What went well today,” class scale and class goal “What does our class look like at 7?”

7 · Progress review: preparation and support agreement

Preparation for students with pieces of work, sentence starters and self-assessment, plus a support agreement.

8 · Conversation reflection

For self- or peer observation during teaching practice: speaking time, question types, feedback levels, way of speaking and stance.

E · Antinomies

Tensions in learning conversations

Anyone who leads learning conversations has to weigh up between poles. Move the sliders and think about where you stand in a specific situation. Below each slider is what the sources say.

counselinggrading
support ↔ assessment

Counseling and grading should be kept as separate as possible (Kremers & Schlüter, 2018). At the same time, the same teacher gives grades (Lötscher et al., 2023). → Clarify roles openly: will this conversation be graded, what will happen with what is said?

pursuing my own goallistening openly
intention ↔ openness

Teachers almost always have a goal, but hidden intentions put people off (Juul & Jensen, 2019). → Make your intention transparent, then stay curious.

acknowledging the problemlooking at solutions
problem ↔ solution

Rushing to the solution loses the person (Bamberger, 2022). Talking about problems for too long leads to defensiveness (Berg & Shilts, 2005). → First acknowledge and narrow it down, then ask about exceptions and goals.

praisinginforming
encouragement ↔ information

Praise directed at the person has little effect on learning (Wisniewski & Zierer, 2017), compliments on specific actions strengthen the relationship and confidence (Berg & Shilts, 2005). → Acknowledge specifically and combine it with information about learning.

letting them find itexplaining and advising
coaching ↔ instruction

Their own solutions carry further (Kremers & Schlüter, 2018), but teaching without explaining is hardly conceivable (Brüning & Siewert, 2026). → First the student’s ideas, then targeted explanation if it is wanted or missing.

personalprofessionally distant
closeness ↔ distance

Personal language strengthens relationships; private matters don’t replace them (Juul & Jensen, 2019). Coaching is not therapy (Kremers & Schlüter, 2018). → Be visible as a person, know the limits of your role and refer onward when needed.

The wording of the tensions is my own synthesis of the sources cited.

Wrap-up

Key points and reflection

Seven key points

1
A learning conversation is a dialogue among unequals: the teacher is responsible for the process, the student for the content.
2
There are different formats, from brief feedback to the progress review, and each needs a clear setting.
3
Feedback on process and self-regulation is rare but especially valuable. Praise directed at the person does little for learning.
4
Self-efficacy grows through own successes, close role models, credible encouragement and interpreted feelings.
5
Ask instead of tell: exceptions, scales and “instead” questions open up a view of solutions.
6
Criticism can be phrased as a wish for the future, with benefits, confidence and a trial run.
7
Every learning conversation ends with a concrete next step the student has chosen.
Food for thought
  • Which sentence do I say to students most often in class? Which level is it on?
  • When did I last ask about an exception instead of the problem?
  • To whom could I send good news home this week?
  • Where do I pursue an intention in conversations that I don’t say out loud?
“What is your next step?”

This question belongs at the end of every learning conversation. It hands responsibility for learning back to where learning happens.
