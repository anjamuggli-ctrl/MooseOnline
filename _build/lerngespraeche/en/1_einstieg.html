Getting started

Starting point: three sentences, three conversations

“Good job!”
“You just need to practice more.”
“Any questions? No? Good.”

Sentences like these are said every day, often in passing. They are meant kindly, but they say little about what went well, how to move on and who plans the next step. This page asks: How do I talk with students about learning so that they keep thinking for themselves, trust in their abilities and know what to do next?

Line of thought · three questions for every learning conversation
1
Who is talking? How much room do students have for their view, their questions and their ideas? → Dialogue
2
About what? About the task, the learning process, the regulation of learning, or the person? → Levels
3
Where to? Does the conversation end with a concrete next step the student has chosen? → Models

Guiding idea

A learning conversation is a conversation about a person’s learning, held with that person. It makes their current level and progress visible, clarifies expectations and prompts them to think about their own thinking. The teacher is responsible for a good course of the conversation. Students remain the experts on their own learning and have a say in how it continues.
