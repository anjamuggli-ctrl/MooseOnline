Introduction

Starting point: who is supposed to learn something here?

“The class is weak.”
“He’s just lazy.”
“She simply can’t sit still.”

Sentences like these come quickly. They sound like descriptions of learning prerequisites, but they are often already interpretations and judgments. This page asks: What do learners actually bring with them, how can I capture this with as little bias as possible, and what does that mean for my teaching?

Line of thought · three questions before any planning
1
What do the learners bring with them? Prior knowledge, strategies, motivation, emotions, development, experiences with relationships. → INVO model
2
How do I know this, and where might I be mistaken? → Perception
3
What does this mean for goals, content, methods and support? → Analysis of learning conditions

Guiding idea

Learning prerequisites are not fixed characteristics of learners. They become apparent in the interplay of person, task, group and environment. If you want to take them into account, you need to capture them carefully, check your own perception and design teaching to fit.

A · Basic understanding

Learning always builds on what is already there

According to the constructivist understanding of learning, learners actively build up structures of knowledge and skills. This never happens without prerequisites: existing structures are adapted, expanded and restructured (PH Luzern, 2022, p. 6).

What you see and what you don’t

Own illustration based on PH Luzern, 2022, Fig. 1, p. 5 (based on Reusser, 2009).

Surface and deep structure
Learning processes take place in learners’ minds. Which actions of the teacher lead to which learning processes can only be inferred indirectly and requires careful interpretation.PH Luzern, 2022, p. 5 (based on Reusser, 2009)
Line of thought
Most learning prerequisites belong to the deep structure. What we see (someone looking out of the window, handing in blank sheets) is only a clue. This leads to a basic stance for the whole page: observations are hypotheses, not diagnoses.
Three basic needs
For long-term motivation, experiencing competence (“I can …”), autonomy (“I can have a say”) and relatedness (“I belong”) is fundamental.PH Luzern, 2022, pp. 6–7
A · A model

The INVO model: five areas of individual prerequisites

Hasselhorn & Gold (2022) summarize the most important individual prerequisites for successful learning in the INVO model. It builds on the “good information processing” model by Pressley, Borkowski & Schneider (1989) and adds volition and emotions.

The five gears
cognitive
  • selective attention and working memory
  • prior knowledge
  • strategies and metacognitive regulation
motivational-volitional
  • motivation and self-concept
  • volition and learning-related emotions
Hasselhorn & Gold, 2022, pp. 67–68
Why gears?
The authors chose this representation deliberately: the gears are connected, but as drawn, they cannot turn. This shows that, despite much detailed knowledge, we still don’t know exactly how the prerequisites need to work together (pp. 67–68). The model is therefore a map, not a recipe.
Practice
The model works well as an observation grid: when learning stalls, which gear might be the cause? Is prior knowledge or a strategy missing, is confidence lacking, or is the person emotionally blocked at the moment?
Differences between and within people
Learners differ from one another (interindividual differences), and the same person does not learn equally successfully in every situation (intraindividual variability). Prerequisites also change with development.Hasselhorn & Gold, 2022, pp. 66–68
A · Fit

Fit instead of deficit

Whether learning prerequisites become a problem depends on how well demands and prerequisites fit together. This idea connects several sources.

Own illustration.

Vygotsky: the ZPD
The zone of proximal development lies between what a child can solve independently and what they can achieve with support from adults or more capable peers. Higher mental processes first emerge in shared activities and are then internalized.Woolfolk, 2014, ch. 2.4
Learning difficulties as a problem of fit
Learning difficulties arise at the threshold between existing abilities and those still to be acquired, when demands are out of proportion to individual prerequisites. They result from an interaction between person and environment.Hagmann-von Arx et al., 2025, p. 3 (based on Heimlich, 2022; Gold, 2018)
Person and environment
The diagnosis–support cycle also understands learning and behavioral difficulties as a problem of fit between individual prerequisites, activity and participation, and environmental factors such as the quality of teaching.Tönnissen et al., 2024, p. 305
Line of thought · What follows from this?
Taking learning prerequisites into account means looking at both sides of the scale: What does the person bring, and what does my teaching demand? The fit can often be improved more easily on the demand side (task, access, time, help) than on the side of the person.
Difficulties in learning arise in all learning processes for all learners.Heimlich (2022, p. 30), quoted in Hagmann-von Arx et al., 2025, p. 2
