D · Toolbox

Tools for capturing learning prerequisites

No single tool provides a complete picture. What makes sense is a combination of several perspectives: what learners show, what they say themselves, and what others observe (own synthesis of the sources).

Assess prior knowledge

  • a short warm-up task or prior-knowledge test
  • a concept map or mind map on the topic
  • “What do you already know? What would you like to know?”
based on Nückles & Hipp, 2026; PH Luzern, 2022

Read learning traces

  • errors as windows onto thinking
  • learning journal, solution paths, drafts
  • How does someone proceed, not just: What is the result?
cf. learning support

Observe deliberately

  • a few clearly defined characteristics
  • several short windows
  • note descriptively, interpret later
based on Fisseni, 2004

Ask the learners

  • self-assessment of ability and interest
  • “What helps you learn? What gets in your way?”
  • take learners seriously as experts on their own learning
own synthesis

Involve others

  • exchange with the special education teacher, German-as-a-second-language teacher, homeroom teacher
  • conversations with parents
  • observe data protection and confidentiality
based on Marti & Camenisch, 2018

Read the group

  • What stage is the class in?
  • Which norms apply without being spoken?
  • When and during what does it go well?
based on Stahl, 2017; Wettstein & Scherzinger, 2022

E · Antinomies

Seven tensions

Taking learning prerequisites into account means weighing between poles. Move the sliders and consider where you stand in a specific situation. Below each slider is what the sources say about it.

knowing preciselynot pinning down
knowing ↔ labeling

Without knowledge there is no fit; with categories there is a risk of pinning people down. Learning prerequisites are changeable (Hasselhorn & Gold, 2022), disruptions are not static characteristics (Wettstein & Scherzinger, 2022, p. 34). → Word findings as snapshots, with date and situation.

using categoriesdistrusting categories
orientation ↔ distortion

Stereotypes orient and distort at the same time (Thomas, 2006); they run automatically (Werth et al., 2020). → Describe before interpreting; deliberately look for contradicting observations.

the personthe environment
Where does the problem lie?

Disability and learning difficulties arise through interaction (ICF; Sturm, 2024; Hagmann-von Arx et al., 2025). → Always also ask: What could I change about my teaching?

providing supportavoiding stigma
measure ↔ stigmatization

Adapted learning goals help but can stigmatize and reinforce social inequality (Hagmann-von Arx et al., 2025, p. 13). → Review measures regularly, communicate transparently with parents.

sharing informationprotecting data
need for information ↔ data protection

Good planning needs information; learners have a right to protection (Zumsteg et al., 2018; Marti & Camenisch, 2018). → Pass on only what is necessary for learning; anonymize.

differentiatingpreserving what is shared
individual ↔ class

Every form of differentiation has downsides (Kress, 2019); the group is itself a learning prerequisite (Stahl, 2017). → Combine guided and natural differentiation; plan shared phases deliberately.

measuring against the normmeasuring against progress
social ↔ individual reference norm

Schools must compare achievement and should, at the same time, honor individual development. For motivation and self-concept, what counts is whether progress becomes visible (cf. Hasselhorn & Gold, 2022, ch. 2). → Give individual feedback on learning progress, even where the grade compares.

The wording of the tensions is an own synthesis of the sources mentioned.

Conclusion

Key messages and questions for reflection

Seven key messages

1
Learning always builds on what is already there, and most of it lies in the deep structure.
2
The INVO model organizes five areas: attention and working memory, prior knowledge, strategies, motivation and self-concept, volition and emotion.
3
Prior knowledge is the most important starting point, and teachers often overestimate it.
4
Difficulties are a question of the fit between demands and prerequisites.
5
Terms such as learning disorder or disability say a lot about the understanding with which one looks at learners.
6
Our perception can be distorted: describe first, then interpret, then evaluate.
7
The analysis of learning conditions only pays off with the third column: What follows from it for my planning?
Food for thought
  • Which learning prerequisite of a person did I recently misjudge? How did I notice?
  • Which category do I often use in the staff room? What would I describe instead?
  • Where in my teaching does the hurdle lie in the task rather than in the person?
  • What stage is my class in right now?
“Who is supposed to learn something here?”

The question behind the analysis of learning conditions is never fully answered. It remains an attitude: curious about the learners, cautious with one’s own judgment.
